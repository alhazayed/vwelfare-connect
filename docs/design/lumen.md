# Lumen

*Light gathered in the dark, held behind glass.*

Lumen is a movement of luminous depth. Its ground is a deep night ink, and on it soft fields of light bloom and overlap: violet, aqua and a warm peach, each diffused until its edge is only a suggestion. These are not shapes but presences. Over them lies a strict, patient grid of tiny points, a measuring field that brightens wherever light passes beneath it, so that order and glow are always seen together. The work must feel meticulously crafted, the blur and the grid tuned against each other with painstaking care.

Material is glass. Rounded translucent panels float above the light, frosting what lies beneath them, edged by a single hair-fine line of brightness. They hold nothing and reveal nothing in detail; they only promise that something private is safely contained. The contrast between the soft, uncontrolled color and the hard, precise glass edge is the movement's tension, and it should be resolved with master-level execution.

Color is used sparingly but at full saturation: three lights and one ink, plus a single coral spark set slightly apart. Every hue must be calibrated so that the dark never turns muddy and the light never turns neon. The result should look like the product of deep expertise in color, light and screen.

Rhythm comes from concentric rings radiating faintly from one source of light, spaced with mathematical calm, a signal sent outward and received. Scale moves from the vast, out-of-focus bloom to the exact one-pixel point.

Typography is light and wide, set in small sizes against large darkness, Latin and Arabic paired in the same thin weight. Words are labels on an instrument panel, never paragraphs. Everything must breathe; everything must look labored over by someone at the absolute top of the craft.
